Resources

White papers on CRM and AI in sales.

Practical reading for sales leaders: why CRMs still leave reps doing the admin, what slow replies cost, how CRM AI is really priced, and the controls to expect before you let AI act for your team. Free to download.

White paper 01

The CRM that waits

Why most CRMs leave sales teams doing the work, and what an AI-native CRM changes.

Most sales teams don’t have a CRM problem. They have a typing problem. This paper sets out the four things traditional CRMs, including their new AI features, still leave with the sales team: inbound inquiries that wait too long, deal records that depend on reps typing them up, AI that is bolted on and metered, and value that only arrives after a project.

It then describes a different model, a CRM built around AI agents that do the work under rules the team sets, with a worked example and six questions to ask any CRM vendor.

White paper 02

Speed to lead

What happens to an inquiry at 9pm, and what a slow first reply costs.

Every sales leader says inbound leads are their best leads. Far fewer can say what happens to one that arrives at 9pm on a Thursday. This paper walks through a typical evening inquiry hour by hour, sets out what the research says about response times, and explains why slow replies are a design problem, not a people problem.

It includes a simple way to estimate what slow replies cost your team, what a good first reply looks like, and how AI agents can answer within minutes without taking control away from you.

White paper 03

The true cost of CRM AI

Add-ons, credits and markups, versus bringing your own AI key at cost.

AI is now part of every CRM sales pitch, and it’s much harder to work out what it will actually cost. This paper compares the ways vendors charge for AI: per-user add-ons, credits that run out and per-action pricing. It looks at the hidden costs in each, from budgets you can’t forecast to features that pause at the end of an allowance.

It sets out the alternative, connecting your own AI account at the provider’s published rate, with the trade-offs stated plainly and a worksheet for comparing vendors on your own volumes.

White paper 04

The self-updating pipeline

Why forecasts drift, and safe rules for AI that updates records.

A forecast is only as good as the deals behind it, and the deals are only as good as the last time someone updated them. This paper explains why forecasts drift: records that lag the conversation, close dates that go stale and risks that are remembered rather than recorded.

It then sets out seven rules that make AI updates safe enough to rely on, from never overwriting a person to quoting the buyer when flagging risk, and the questions to ask any vendor before letting AI edit your CRM.

White paper 05

AI you can trust in sales

Approvals, audit trails, no invented facts and one pause switch.

The most useful AI in sales is the AI that acts: it replies to a buyer, books a meeting, updates a deal. That is exactly what makes sales leaders nervous. This paper sets out six controls every team should expect before letting AI act on its behalf, from approval-first autonomy to a complete audit trail.

It finishes with a three-stage path for moving from drafts to trusted autonomy at your own pace, and seven questions to ask any vendor.
